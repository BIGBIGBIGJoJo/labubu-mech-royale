# Authorized nearby-resource interruption

Written before implementation. The user explicitly authorized collecting nearby upgrades safely and then resuming pursuit. This candidate starts from `919f826` (official 4.21) and changes optional resource movement during pursuit. A separately reviewed, mathematically equivalent terrain endpoint cache is included under `CACHE_PLAN.md`; no spacing change is included. Nearest living mech/NPC selection, physical firing, nearby-enemy reload/healing gates and urgent-zone authority remain unchanged.

## Admission and limits

Consider currently listed useful cubes and a weapon that fills an empty second slot. A cube need not be attributed to our kill. Below the ten-cube stat cap it provides power; above the cap, optional travel requires a currently visible pickup cluster to cross an upgrade threshold. Do not detour for crates, NPC farming, repair captures, unseen remembered items or weapon replacement. Existing already-in-reach take/collection behavior needs no lease.

An elective detour requires an active pursuit target, no urgent rotation or active recovery, HP at least 55%, no non-zone damage in the preceding three seconds, a grounded bot and an available map. Incomplete current threat visibility vetoes entry. Reuse existing resource value, vertical, blacklist and mech-exposure formulas, evaluating exposure at the actual pickup endpoint and valuing only the currently listed cubes collectible there. A second weapon requires an actual empty inventory slot. On both collection and return segments, reject moving closer inside a listed NPC's configured aggro range plus body radius, or inside forty metres plus body radius of a mech. These are current modeled checks, not a guarantee against future enemies or projectiles.

Use walking speed for estimates; if any laser is held, always apply its live charge movement multiplier, even before Combat starts charging that decision. Do not fund the bound with boost energy. Form a local rejoin anchor on the current pursuit-goal direction, at most one second of this conservative walking speed and at most ten metres away. Require a body-sized clear ground endpoint inside the item's official pickup radius, and verify direct supported self→anchor, self→pickup and pickup→anchor segments. Count currently listed cubes collectible at that endpoint and use their uncapped body growth for endpoint, return-anchor and return-segment clearance. Check both endpoint and anchor against the future circle at conservative total trip time plus the existing four-second safety margin, including the enlarged body and eight-metre margin. `navigate` returns steering, not route cost; it is not used to invent an ETA.

This growth check applies to every candidate, including a weapon pickup co-located with cubes. Use the cube pickup radius and vertical reach for incidental cube collection, independently of the weapon take radius. Cube resource value and upgrade-threshold admission remain specific to cube candidates; incidental growth does not turn a weapon candidate into a cube farm.

Place the endpoint at pickup radius minus two metres from the item centre, toward the bot. This leaves half a metre of pickup margin when the unchanged navigation arrival tolerance stops up to 1.5 metres before the endpoint.

For a newly acquired laser, its equip/fire cooldown prevents immediate charging. Price return distance covered during `max(loot.equip_s, laser.cooldown)` at the current conservative walking speed, then any remaining return distance at the laser charge movement multiplier. Collection-leg speed is unchanged. Other newly acquired weapons add no charge-movement penalty.

Engineering limits for this first candidate are at most **2 seconds extra modeled travel** relative to that same verified reference segment and at most **3 seconds total modeled travel via the item to the anchor**. A fixed **3-second lease** bounds the movement interruption. These are policy caps, not game constants. Their implementation must demonstrate real recorded admissions; failure to find any is evidence to revisit the plan rather than claim a working feature.

The original private NPC drops at 3.9 s were approximately 64–68 m away, and even their closest later observation remained 36–39 m away with energy near the boost floor and recent incoming damage. This plan does not claim that detour was safe or should pass. Further diagnostics already showed substantial acquisition and regeneration in no-target windows; those existing paths must continue working.

## Continuation and return

The lease stores the current item, nearest target ID, original expiry and observed cube/inventory state. It cannot renew on retargeting. Every action must still have the item currently listed and useful and pass current eligibility, exposure, zone and direct geometry/ETA checks. Abort immediately on urgent rotation, recovery, a changed nearest enemy, damage, invalid/absent item, lost route, unsafe exposure or expiry. A successful cube-count increase or weapon acquisition completes the lease. Both completion and abort return to freshly computed nearest pursuit on that decision; no walk back to a stale anchor is required.

After any lease ends, require two seconds of resumed pursuit before another optional interruption. An aborted item also has a six-second detour-only retry delay; this must not blacklist it from ordinary no-target healing/resource behavior. Bound and prune that retry memory. No immediate next-frame lease restart or item-to-item extension is allowed.

Keep the combat target as the nearest enemy throughout. Existing physical checks decide triggers, and both partial reload paths retain the shared nearby gate. A detour never creates permission to fire through geometry or to redirect an unavoidable held-laser release. Ordinary tactical collision, projectile and energy checks remain authoritative.

## Budget and validation

A cube pile therefore cannot consume the weapon's candidate slot. Check the optional deadline while scanning current items, then count the full observed cube cluster and construct endpoints for only those shortlisted items; each cluster scan also checks the deadline. Reuse the existing scorer through a constrained current-items/pickup-only interface with precomputed cluster counts, retaining its ordinary defaults. The optional branch has a six-millisecond cooperative budget, additionally bounded by the full 32 ms policy deadline with at least seventeen milliseconds reserved downstream: `min(start + 15 ms, optional_start + 6 ms)`. Check before world rays and each endpoint/segment query. Unknown or expired optional work yields pursuit; a query already in flight may finish after the deadline and must be measured. Admission and continuation have the same budget.

Before freezing: verify exact nearest target/fire intent, real-map admitted recorded cases, rejected original far-drop case, useful versus duplicate/full-slot pickups, current-only item scope, body/terrain/vertical/range checks, future zone and known/unknown exposure, NPC proximity, worst-case held-laser speed, fixed expiry, completion/abort/no-chain behavior, recovery/rotation precedence, no change to normal no-target loot, observation immutability and deadlines. Run the complete suite and full-policy timing on an active detour branch. Root owns container/private/official validation. Fixed-observation admission is not a counterfactual collection or survival claim.

## Allocation correction from restricted-runtime measurements

The initial three-millisecond slice passed host admission checks but failed all 100 restricted Linux active-fixture checks. Instrumentation found exactly two exposure rays and zero route queries per decision: current geometry was not disproved; the optional budget ended before route validation could begin. A controlled container sweep admitted 0/20 at 3 ms, 1/20 at 4 ms, 20/20 at 5 ms, and 20/20 at 6 ms. The 5 ms sample measured a 4.555 ms maximum optional stage and 24.403 ms maximum full policy; the 6 ms sample measured 5.142 ms and 24.698 ms respectively.

The revised local cap is six milliseconds for measured headroom, with the same absolute start+15ms cutoff and seventeen-millisecond downstream reserve. No safety predicate, geometric model, lease rule, target choice, full-policy deadline or candidate count changes. Actual-Bot allocation tests cover both the six-millisecond local cap and a late-entry case where the absolute cutoff takes precedence. Root will rebuild and repeat restricted validation on the resulting source before any private or official test.

An affected dense-loot check subsequently exposed a separate growth omission: after a cube candidate failed enlarged-body clearance, a co-located weapon could pass at the original body size. The narrow correction counts endpoint cubes for weapon candidates too, using the cube radius. Co-located laser-plus-three-cube and distinct cube/weapon-radius regressions cover the correction. Diagnostics run on the earlier `2413d274` snapshot remain pre-correction evidence.

The corrected source passes 262 unit tests, including 43 resource tests. Only affected host timing cases were rerun: the recorded active observation admitted 100/100 calls (full-policy maximum 22.384 ms, optional maximum 3.941 ms); the synthetic 256-cube co-located pile rejected 100/100 after applying incidental growth (full maximum 23.570 ms, optional maximum 5.391 ms). No call exceeded 50 ms. The earlier 2,543-observation replay below was not rerun and remains historical three-millisecond-source evidence. Exact-image restricted and fresh private validation are root-owned checks.

## Initial host validation (three-millisecond allocation)

The combined source passes 258 unit tests, including 39 focused resource tests and the independently owned cache tests. A replay of 2,543 fixed observations from four private diagnostics admitted 25 consecutive secondary-laser decisions from 4.2 to 6.6 seconds, retaining the nearest enemy and the original 7.2-second lease expiry. First admission estimated 2.785 seconds total travel and 1.931 seconds extra travel. The other three traces admitted no detours under these gates. All replay decisions were below 50 ms (maximum 31.475 ms). These observations were not advanced using the new actions, so they do not establish eventual collection or improved results.

A sanitized positive observation with complete relevant incoming controller state reproduced admission in 19/20 fresh real-map controllers, with one optional-budget rejection and maximum full-policy time 21.055 ms. A further instrumented 100-call sample admitted 99 times and conservatively rejected one budget-limited check; full-policy maximum was 18.575 ms. A synthetic 256-cube pile exercised the bounded scan and cluster work in 100 real-map calls: no stale admission, full-policy maximum 23.496 ms, resource-phase maximum 4.343 ms. The three-millisecond budget is cooperative and can overrun by an already-running geometry query. None of these host checks substitutes for the restricted container or an official score.
